Evaluation cadence
All three configs specify evaluation_interval_epochs=2. However:
                            PPO          TES no filter   TES filtered
Received simulator rows     4.01408B      4.00000B        4.00000B
Collection/training cycles  245           2422            2437

Received rows/evaluation    32.768M       ~3.303M         ~3.283M
Actual evaluations          123           1211            1219

PPO fills 8.192M rollout slots per rank on two ranks each cycle. TES refreshes
ceil(2.048M/3)=682,667 valid replay rows per rank; received rows are larger
because not every received agent slot becomes a valid insertion. TES's
received-row intervals above are run-wide means, not exact step triggers.

PPO evaluation directories begin epoch_000002_step_32768000 and finish
epoch_000245_step_4014080000. The odd final cycle gets a completion evaluation.
PPO W&B epoch=490 is SUM-reduced across two ranks; it means 245 actual cycles.
TES's logged epoch is the actual cycle count. Do not compare displayed epoch
numbers directly.

Both use carla_fast, 250 scenarios, 500 steps, seed42, mean actions, dt=.1 and
30m goal spacing. Both train at dt=.3 with training goal spacing20–200m.
Both use two RTX A6000s, GPU-resident experience, compilation, bf16 AMP and
matching policy-backbone settings. PPO has actor/value networks; TES has an
actor, twin online Q critics and target critics. PPO microbatch32K versus TES64K
is another native-config difference; this is a workload accounting comparison,
not a contemporaneous same-configuration controlled training experiment.

Why the filtered TES learner remains slower
PPO trained on approximately2.333B retained row-exposures, including3passes.
TES filtered trained on0.788B exposures with1pass/cycle, while evaluating
targets/scores for9.982B candidate visits. Thus filtered TES actually performs
fewer retained training exposures than PPO in this comparison.

PPO records values during collection, computes GAE/returns once per rollout
using the CUDA recurrence, filters scalar advantages once, and reuses retained
advantages/returns over3passes. Its value-network collection work is included
in collection time. It does not need new full-candidate online-Q scoring
for each replay minibatch.

TES prepares current bootstrap targets and online twin-Q TD-error scores for
every candidate batch before selection. Its historical full-candidate target
and score costs total5.078h, exceeding PPO's entire1.664h learner time. Only
after this does it train the retained subset. The recent bounded two-GPU probe
independently measured7.526s/cycle for those target+score passes, matching the
historical7.501s/cycle estimate. This explains most of the remaining learner gap.

Checkpoint difference
PPO checkpoint_interval=500; it finishes at245cycles, saving at completion
and close. Its evaluation branch does not call save_evaluation_checkpoint.
TES checkpoint_interval=null does not suppress evaluation-triggered saves:
every scheduled benchmark calls save_evaluation_checkpoint(), which saves a
learner checkpoint. Its separate cost has not been isolated.

Fair future comparison
Use a shared GLOBAL RECEIVED-STEP evaluation trigger every32,768,000 steps,
plus final evaluation, with the same benchmark and a separately matched
checkpoint policy. TES interval20cycles approximates PPO's received-step
spacing but is not exact because valid-insertion rates change. Keep learner,
collection, benchmark and checkpoint timings separate. No config was changed
or new training run launched for this comparison.
